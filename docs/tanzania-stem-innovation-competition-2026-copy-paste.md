## Description (paste into: Description)

The **Tanzania STEM Innovation Competition 2026** is a nationwide programme led by **Adilisha Child and Youth Development** through the **Adilisha STEM Labs** digital platform. It aims to engage up to **200 public secondary schools** across all regions of Tanzania.

Each participating school forms **one official team of up to 10 students** (with a **minimum of 70% female students** on the team). Before the school can compete in later stages, it must register **at least 75 students** on the platform and meet the **institutional gender-balance rules** set out in the terms.

Teams identify a **real social or community problem** and design a **tangible, working prototype** using STEM concepts and **locally available materials** (see terms for the materials rule and evidence requirements).

The competition runs in **eight structured phases**: registration and student onboarding; idea submission; regional shortlisting of ideas; prototype development; prototype shortlisting; mentor onboarding; prototype refinement; and the **national pitching and awards event** (planned for **December 2026**).

## Terms and conditions (paste into: Terms and conditions)

**1. Eligibility (school type)**  
Participation is limited to **public secondary schools** registered in the **United Republic of Tanzania**. Private schools, international schools, and informal groups are **not eligible** unless the organisers publish a separate invitation.

**2. Official school representative**  
Each school must have **one teacher account** (school representative) responsible for accuracy of school data, student records, uploads, and all competition submissions. The representative must accept these terms on behalf of the school.

**3. Registration volume (75+ students)**  
Each school must register **at least 75 students** on the official portal by the **registration closing date**. This is a **minimum institutional requirement**. Late registration after the published deadline **will not** be accepted. The platform may block progression to the next phase until this threshold is met.

**4. Team composition (10 students, 70% female)**  
Each school nominates **one team of up to 10 students** drawn from its registered students. **At least 70% of the team must be female students.** All team members must be **actively enrolled** at the school, listed on the portal, and able to provide **valid student identification** if requested.

**5. Accuracy of information**  
Schools must provide truthful school details (including region/district), accurate student names and class levels, and honest declarations about projects and materials. **Falsified documents, misrepresentation of gender or enrolment, or plagiarised work** may lead to disqualification.

**6. Project focus and integrity**  
Projects must address a **documented community problem** relevant to Tanzania and demonstrate **STEM-based reasoning**. Solutions should be **safe**, **feasible**, and appropriate for a school environment. **Hazardous chemicals, unsafe electrical work, or weapons-related projects** are not permitted.

**7. Local materials (75%)**  
Teams should use **at least 75% locally sourced materials** (by cost or by mass/volume—whichever method the organisers specify in the submission form). Schools may be asked to submit a **materials log** or **project diary** listing components, approximate cost, and origin (local vs imported). Organisers may verify entries through spot checks or regional visits.

**8. Submissions and deadlines**  
Unless the competition administrators **explicitly reopen** a submission, each phase closes on the published date. **No late submissions** will be accepted for that phase. Drafts should be completed before the deadline.

**9. Judging and progression**  
Shortlisting and awards are based on published criteria (for example: **STEM soundness**, **community relevance**, **creativity**, **feasibility**, **use of local materials**, **prototype functionality**, and **quality of presentation**). **Judges’ and organisers’ decisions on progression and prizes are final**, subject only to correction of proven administrative error.

**10. Media, data, and consent**  
Participating schools consent to **reasonable use of anonymised or attributed project summaries, photos, and pitch recordings** for education, reporting, and public awareness, in line with national child-protection norms. Personal data is handled according to the platform privacy notice.

**11. Code of conduct**  
Participants, teachers, mentors, and judges must treat one another with **respect**. **Harassment, bullying, hate speech, or cheating** may result in removal of content, loss of eligibility, or reporting to relevant authorities where required.

**12. Changes**  
The organisers may issue **clarifications or minor updates** to schedules and forms. **Material rule changes** will be communicated through the portal and, where possible, with reasonable notice.

## Participant instructions (paste into: Instructions — optional but recommended)

**Who does what**

- **Teacher (school representative):** completes school profile, uploads or enters student records, confirms the school meets the **75+ registered students** rule, selects the **10-member team** (respecting the **70% female** rule), and submits official forms each phase.
- **Students:** take part in problem identification, research, building, testing, and (where invited) pitching. **Students do not log in** unless the platform later adds student accounts—follow your teacher’s guidance.

**Before you start**

1. Read the **terms and conditions** in full.  
2. Gather **school registration details** and a **list of participating students** (full name, sex, class/form).  
3. Plan how you will meet the **local materials** rule and keep simple **receipts, photos, or notes** as evidence.

**Getting help**

Use in-platform notices and your regional contact (if provided). For technical issues with login or uploads, use the support channel shown on the portal.

## Phase descriptions (paste each block into the matching phase’s Description field)

> **Note:** Your Laravel default phases are: Registration → Idea Submission → Idea Shortlisting → Prototype Development → Prototype Shortlisting → Mentor Onboarding → Prototype Modification → Pitch & Winner. Titles below match that flow; rename phases in admin if you prefer “Regional shortlisting” wording in the UI.

### Phase 1 — Registration (`registration`)

**What happens**  
Schools create or confirm their **teacher account**, complete **school profile** (name, region, district, contact person), and accept the **competition terms**.

**Teacher responsibilities**

- **Upload or register students** using the format required by the portal (names, sex, class/form, and any optional fields such as age or phone for SMS).  
- Ensure data is **accurate**—typos and duplicates slow down verification.  
- Monitor the **registered student count** until the school reaches **at least 75 students**.  
- After the threshold is met, **select exactly one team of up to 10 students** for the competition, ensuring **at least 70% are female**.  
- Designate **roles** if the system allows (for example: team captain, documentation lead)—at least one adult remains accountable for submissions.

**Eligibility checks**  
The organiser may **review** new school accounts (for example, to confirm **public secondary** status). Schools remain **pending** until approved where applicable.

**Close of phase**  
When registration closes on **[date]**, **no new students** may be added unless the administrators reopen registration. Schools below **75 registered students** may be **ineligible** for the next phase.

---

### Phase 2 — Idea submission (`idea_submission`)

**What happens**  
Eligible schools submit **one idea** per school: the **problem**, **proposed STEM-based solution**, **community and region**, disciplines involved, and optional **documents or images**.

**Rules**

- **One submission per school** (draft allowed until final submit).  
- After final submission, the idea is **locked** unless an admin reopens it.  
- Content must be **original** to the team’s work at that school.

**Deadline**  
All ideas must be submitted by **[date/time, timezone]**.

---

### Phase 3 — Idea shortlisting / regional review (`idea_shortlisting`)

**What happens**  
Assigned **judges** review ideas against the rubric (STEM quality, impact, creativity, feasibility, alignment with local materials approach). The system supports **regional scoring** and ranking.

**School experience**  
Schools **do not** edit ideas in this phase. **Shortlisted** schools are notified through the portal (and email if enabled) and move forward; others stop at this stage but retain access for certificates or future cycles where applicable.

**Outcome**  
Regional or national shortlists are published according to the **admin configuration** (for example, top **N** teams per region).

---

### Phase 4 — Prototype development (`prototype_development`)

**What happens**  
Shortlisted teams **build a working prototype** aligned with the approved idea. Teachers submit the **prototype package** (description, photos or short video, materials breakdown, self-assessment of how it works).

**Evidence**

- Maintain a simple **materials log**: item, source (local shop, scrap, school lab, etc.), estimated cost, and **local vs imported** classification.  
- Aim for **≥75% local materials** as defined in the submission form.

**Deadline**  
Prototype submission by **[date/time]**.

---

### Phase 5 — Prototype shortlisting (`prototype_shortlisting`)

**What happens**  
Judges evaluate prototypes against **functionality**, **safety**, **community fit**, **documentation quality**, and **local materials evidence**. Rankings may be **per region** or **national**, depending on organiser settings.

**Outcome**  
Advancing teams are notified and unlocked for **mentor matching** and refinement.

---

### Phase 6 — Mentor onboarding (`mentor_onboarding`)

**What happens**  
Organisers assign **university or industry mentors** to advancing teams. Mentors can view the school’s **idea and prototype history** and communicate through the **official mentor thread** (not private side channels if the rules require logged communication).

**Teams should**

- Introduce themselves, share current **photos/videos**, list **blockers**, and respond to mentor feedback within agreed timelines.

---

### Phase 7 — Prototype modification / refinement (`prototype_modification`)

**What happens**  
Teams **improve** the prototype using mentor feedback—better reliability, usability, cost, or safety. Updated descriptions, media, and materials logs may be submitted **once** per rules (draft until deadline).

**Principle**  
Refinement should show **measurable improvement**, not a completely unrelated project.

**Deadline**  
Refined package due **[date/time]**.

---

### Phase 8 — Pitch & winner / national event (`pitch_winner`)

**What happens**  
Finalists prepare a **short live or recorded pitch** (format announced by organisers). Judges score **presentation**, **impact story**, **STEM depth**, and **prototype demonstration**.

**Logistics**  
Schools may be asked to **confirm attendance**, travel plans, and chaperone details for the **December 2026** national event.

**Awards**  
Winners and runners-up are announced on **[date]**; prizes and certificates follow organiser communication.
